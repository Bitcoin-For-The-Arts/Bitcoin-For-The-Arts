# ECF Partnership Proposal

**Subject: Proposal for Partnership: Bitcoin Financial Literacy Programming for Entertainment Professionals**

Dear Entertainment Community Fund Financial Wellness Team,

I hope this message finds you well. My name is Dion Wilson, and I am reaching out as both a working entertainment professional and the founder of a nonprofit dedicated to artist financial empowerment.

As a member of Actors' Equity Association and former member of the American Guild of Musical Artists (AGMA), I have spent my career in the performing arts — performing in the national tour of The Lion King with AEA, and dancing with the Dance Theatre of Harlem and Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater under AGMA. Through these experiences, I have witnessed firsthand the financial challenges entertainment professionals face: irregular income cycles, high transaction fees on payments, limited access to modern financial tools, and difficulty building long-term savings during periods between engagements.

These experiences led me to found Bitcoin for the Arts, Inc. (BFTA), a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt nonprofit based in New York City. BFTA is the first nonprofit paying artists in Bitcoin, and our mission is to equip creators with the financial knowledge and tools to build sovereignty over their careers and earnings. I am writing to propose a collaboration with the Entertainment Community Fund's Financial Wellness Program to bring Bitcoin financial literacy education to your members and program participants.

**Why Bitcoin Education Belongs in Financial Wellness**

The Entertainment Community Fund's Financial Wellness Program already recognizes that entertainment professionals need specialized financial guidance that accounts for the realities of freelance and contract-based careers. Bitcoin and digital payment technologies are an increasingly relevant part of that landscape. Whether or not an individual chooses to hold Bitcoin, understanding how digital currencies, self-custody, the Lightning Network, and decentralized payments work is becoming essential financial literacy — particularly for artists who work across borders, receive payments from multiple sources, and need low-fee options for transferring money quickly.

This is not about speculation or investment advice. It is about practical financial tools: how to receive payments with lower fees, how to build a personal reserve outside of traditional banking, how to protect earnings through self-custody, and how to think about money in a way that serves a career built on irregular income.

**What BFTA Has Built**

BFTA has developed a complete education curriculum designed specifically for artists with no prior Bitcoin knowledge:

- **Level 1 — Foundations:** A four-part series covering What Is Money, Bitcoin for Artists: Unlocking New Creative Freedom, Bitcoin in Practice for Artists, and Protecting Your Bitcoin: Security & Self-Custody Deep Dive
- **Level 2 — Deep Dives:** Sound Money & the Austrian School for Creators, and a three-part Bitcoin & the Creator Economy series covering monetization strategies, audience building, and practical tools
- **All materials are free, open-source, and available at:** bitcoinforthearts.org/education/webinar

These sessions are interactive, beginner-friendly, and built by working artists for working artists. Each webinar includes slide presentations, Q&A, and supplementary reference materials.

Beyond education, BFTA operates a micro-grant program that funds creation costs, production expenses, studio time, and travel for US-based projects across visual arts, music, theater, dance, writing, storytelling, and film. Grants are paid in Bitcoin directly to the artist and reviewed quarterly.

Our organization operates with full transparency. We publish all governance documents, board meeting minutes, code of ethics, CSR policy, and financial allocation publicly. Every dollar follows our 55/30/10/5 model: 55% to direct artist grants, 30% to programs including workshops and residencies, 10% to operations, and 5% to a permanent endowment reserve.

**Proposed Collaboration**

I would like to propose the following ways BFTA could contribute to the Entertainment Community Fund's Financial Wellness Program:

**Financial literacy workshops:** BFTA would deliver free webinars or in-person sessions for ECF program participants on practical Bitcoin and digital payment topics. These could be offered as standalone sessions or integrated into existing Financial Wellness Programming as a digital finance module. Topics can be tailored to the specific needs of your participants — for example, sessions focused on managing touring income, cross-border payments for international engagements, or building long-term reserves during periods of steady work.

**Resource library access:** ECF could share BFTA's education materials (webinar recordings, slide decks, reference guides) with program participants as supplementary financial wellness resources. All materials are free and openly licensed.

**Grant program awareness:** ECF participants who are working on creative projects could be made aware of BFTA's micro-grant program as an additional funding resource. We welcome applicants from all performing arts disciplines.

**Co-branded programming:** For deeper collaboration, we could develop co-branded workshop content that aligns BFTA's Bitcoin education with ECF's broader financial wellness curriculum, ensuring the material complements rather than duplicates existing programming.

**What BFTA Would Ask of ECF**

**Promotion and distribution:** Share information about the webinars and resources with ECF program participants through your existing communication channels — newsletters, program materials, website, or direct outreach.

**Feedback and guidance:** ECF's team understands the financial challenges of entertainment professionals better than almost anyone. Your input on which topics would be most relevant and how to frame the material for your audience would be invaluable in making the programming as useful as possible.

**Venue or platform access:** For in-person sessions, access to ECF's facilities or partner venues in New York City would allow us to reach participants directly. For virtual sessions, integration into ECF's existing webinar or workshop infrastructure would simplify logistics.
