## Slide 2 — Let's Start With a Question

**Imagine a world with no money.**

You're an artist. You just painted a beautiful picture. Now you're hungry and want a sandwich.

You walk up to the sandwich maker and say: "I'll trade you this painting for a sandwich."

The sandwich maker says: "Sorry, I don't want a painting. I need new shoes."

Now you have to find someone who wants your painting AND has shoes to trade to the sandwich maker... who then gives you a sandwich.

**That sounds exhausting, right?**

This problem is called the **"coincidence of wants"** — and it's the reason money was invented.

---

## Slide 3 — The Very First Trades: Barter

**Before money existed, people used barter.**

Barter means trading one thing directly for another:
- A farmer trades eggs for a blacksmith's nails
- A fisher trades fish for a weaver's cloth
- An artist trades a carving for a bag of grain

**Barter works... but only sometimes.** It has big problems:

1. **Both people have to want what the other has** (coincidence of wants)
2. **It's hard to split things up** — what if your cow is worth 50 chickens, but you only want 3?
3. **Some things go bad** — you can't save fish for next year
4. **It's hard to compare value** — is one horse worth 10 goats or 20?

People needed something better. They needed **money**.

---

## Slide 4 — How Money Is Born (Nobody Forces It)

**Here is something important: nobody sat down and invented money.**

No king declared it. No government created it. No committee voted on it.

**Money emerged naturally from people trading with each other.**

Over time, people noticed that certain goods were easier to trade than others. They started picking items that most people would accept — not because they were told to, but because it just made life easier.

This is called **emergence** — when something useful appears because of many people making individual choices, not because someone planned it from the top down.

**Think of it like a hiking trail in the woods.** Nobody builds it on purpose. But after hundreds of people walk through the same gap in the trees, a clear path appears. Money works the same way.

---

## Slide 5 — What Makes Good Money?

**Not everything works well as money.** Over thousands of years, humans discovered that the best money has certain qualities:

| Quality | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Durable** | It doesn't rot, break, or disappear | Gold lasts thousands of years; fish does not |
| **Portable** | You can carry it easily | Coins fit in your pocket; a cow does not |
| **Divisible** | You can break it into smaller pieces | You can cut silver into small bits; you can't cut a diamond easily |
| **Fungible** | One unit is the same as another | One gold coin equals any other gold coin of the same weight |
| **Scarce** | It's hard to make more of it | Gold must be mined; you can't just print more |
| **Recognizable** | People can tell it's real | Shells, gold coins, and bills all have distinct looks |

Throughout history, people tried seashells, glass beads, salt, cattle, and many other things. **Gold and silver won out** because they scored highest on all these qualities.

## Slide 6 — Human Action: Why People Do What They Do

**Let's talk about something called "human action."**

Every single thing you do — from eating breakfast to painting a picture to saving your allowance — is an **action** you take to make your life better.

The economist Ludwig von Mises called this **"human action"**: the purposeful behavior of individuals trying to move from a less satisfying situation to a more satisfying one.

**Here's the simple version:**
- You're hungry → you make a sandwich → you feel better
- You're cold → you put on a jacket → you feel warmer
- You want to create → you pick up a paintbrush → you feel fulfilled

Every person has their own **needs, wants, and desires** — and their own **timeline** for when those things matter most. Human action is how we try to satisfy them.

---

## Slide 7 — Why Money Is Essential for Human Action

**Here's where money becomes super important.**

Without money, every person would have to make everything themselves: grow their own food, build their own house, sew their own clothes. That's incredibly hard and slow.

**Money lets people specialize.** Instead of doing everything, you can focus on what you're best at — painting, cooking, coding, building — and then use money to trade for everything else.

This is called **economizing**: using your limited time, energy, and resources in the smartest way possible to satisfy the most needs and wants.

**Money is the tool that makes economizing possible.** It lets every individual:
- **Save** value for the future (your work today can help you tomorrow)
- **Compare** the value of different things (prices tell you what's worth what)
- **Trade** with anyone, anywhere (you don't need them to want your exact product)
- **Plan** for the future (you can budget, invest, and prepare)

**Without sound money, human action is limited. With it, the possibilities are endless.**

## Slide 8 — What Is a Free Market Economy?

**A free market economy is a system where people are free to trade with each other voluntarily.**

No one forces you to buy or sell anything. No one sets the prices. No one tells you what job to have. **You choose.**

**Why does this matter?**

1. **Accountability** — In a free market, if you make a bad product or treat customers poorly, people stop buying from you. You have to earn trust. This holds everyone — individuals and businesses — accountable for their actions.

2. **Best Possible Arrangements** — When people are free to choose, they naturally find the best deals. Buyers seek the best value. Sellers compete to offer it. This process, repeated millions of times, creates arrangements that no single planner could ever design.

3. **Individual Empowerment** — Every person can act on their own needs and timeline. A musician in Nashville and a painter in Lagos can each pursue their craft and trade for what they need — on their own terms, at their own pace.

**A free market isn't chaos. It's coordination through voluntary choice.**

---

## Slide 9 — Prices: The Language of the Free Market

**Prices are how a free market talks.**

When you see a price, it's telling you a story:
- How much work went into making something
- How many people want it
- How rare or available it is

**Prices help every person make smart decisions.** If the price of wood goes up, builders look for alternatives. If the price of an artist's work goes up, it signals that people value it more.

**Without honest prices, people can't economize well.** They can't compare, plan, or save effectively. This is why sound money — money that holds its value and can't be manipulated — is so important for a healthy economy.

## Slide 10 — Building a Circular Economy in Your Community

**Now let's bring this home — to your community.**

A **circular economy** starts when people in the same community trade with each other directly. Instead of sending all your money to faraway corporations, you spend it locally:

- You buy bread from a neighbor's bakery
- The baker buys a painting from a local artist
- The artist hires a local carpenter to frame their work
- The carpenter buys bread from the same bakery

**The money circulates.** It stays in the community. Everyone benefits. Everyone is accountable to their neighbors.

**This is human action at its most powerful:**
- People specializing in what they do best
- Trading freely with one another
- Building trust and reputation
- Economizing together for the most productive outcomes

**A strong community economy is the foundation of a strong society.**

## Slide 11 — How Communities Evolve Through Human Action

**Communities don't stay still. They grow.**

When people in a community trade freely and honestly:
- **New businesses** spring up to fill needs
- **Skills** develop because people invest in getting better
- **Savings** accumulate because sound money holds its value
- **Trust** deepens because everyone is accountable

Over time, the community becomes more **productive** — more goods, more services, more art, more culture, more opportunities for everyone.

**This is organic, bottom-up growth.** It's not planned by a committee. It emerges from the choices of real people — just like money itself once did.

The key ingredient? **Sound money that nobody can cheat.**

## Slide 12 — The Problem with Today's Money

**So what went wrong?**

Today's money (like the U.S. dollar) is called **fiat currency** — money that exists because a government says so.

The problem:
- **Governments can print as much as they want.** When they do, every dollar you hold becomes worth a little less. This is called **inflation**.
- **Your savings lose value over time.** A dollar in 1970 bought what $8 would buy today.
- **It's not fair.** The people closest to the new money (big banks, governments) benefit first. By the time it reaches regular people and artists, prices have already gone up.

**Remember our rule for good money? It should be scarce — hard to make more of.** Fiat money breaks that rule. Anyone with a printing press (or a central bank) can create more.

**This makes it harder for individuals to economize, save, plan, and build.**

## Slide 13 — Enter Bitcoin

**In 2009, something new appeared: Bitcoin.**

Bitcoin was created by a person (or group) using the name **Satoshi Nakamoto**. It was designed to solve the exact problems we just talked about.

**What is Bitcoin?**
- A digital form of money that lives on the internet
- It runs on a network of computers all around the world — no single person or company controls it
- Every transaction is recorded on a public ledger called the **blockchain** — anyone can verify it

**Think of it like a global notebook that everyone can read, but nobody can erase.**

---

## Slide 14 — Why Bitcoin Is Special

**Let's check Bitcoin against our "good money" qualities:**

| Quality | Bitcoin |
|---|---|
| **Durable** | It's digital — it can't rust, break, or decay |
| **Portable** | You can send it anywhere in the world in minutes |
| **Divisible** | 1 Bitcoin can be split into 100,000,000 tiny pieces called **satoshis (sats)** |
| **Fungible** | 1 Bitcoin equals 1 Bitcoin, always |
| **Scarce** | There will only ever be **21 million Bitcoin** — no one can print more |
| **Recognizable** | The blockchain makes it easy to verify any Bitcoin is real |

**Bitcoin is the first form of money in history that is both digital AND truly scarce.**

No government, no bank, no corporation can create more than 21 million. The rules are written in code and enforced by mathematics — not by people who can change their minds.

---

## Slide 15 — Bitcoin's Technology: What's Driving Innovation

**Let's look at the technology that makes Bitcoin work:**

**The Blockchain**
- A shared record book spread across thousands of computers worldwide
- Every 10 minutes, a new "page" (block) is added with recent transactions
- Once a page is added, it can never be changed — this creates trust without trusting any single person

**Decentralization**
- No single company or government runs Bitcoin
- If one computer shuts down, thousands of others keep it running
- This makes Bitcoin incredibly resilient — it has run 24/7 since January 2009

**Open Source**
- Anyone can read Bitcoin's code, verify how it works, and suggest improvements
- It's transparent by design — no secrets, no hidden rules

**The Lightning Network**
- A newer layer built on top of Bitcoin for fast, cheap, everyday payments
- Makes it practical to buy a coffee or tip an artist with sats
- Enables instant transactions anywhere in the world

## Slide 16 — Bitcoin and Human Action: The Next Level

**Now let's connect all the dots.**

Remember: **human action** is every individual working to fulfill their needs, wants, and desires on their own timeline.

**Sound money** is the tool that makes this possible by letting people save, trade, compare value, and plan.

**Bitcoin takes human action to the next level because:**

1. **True Ownership** — You hold your Bitcoin. No bank can freeze it. No government can confiscate it. Your money is yours.

2. **Global Access** — Anyone with a phone and internet can use Bitcoin — whether you're an artist in New York, a musician in Nairobi, or a craftsperson in São Paulo.

3. **Savings That Hold Value** — With only 21 million ever, Bitcoin can't be inflated away. Your work today is preserved for tomorrow.

4. **Permissionless Trade** — You don't need a bank account, credit check, or government approval to send or receive Bitcoin. You just need the network.

5. **Transparent and Verifiable** — Every transaction is public. Every rule is visible. Trust is built into the math, not into promises.

## Slide 18 — Economizing at the Next Level

**Economizing means using your limited resources in the smartest way possible.**

Bitcoin introduces new tools for economizing that didn't exist before:

- **Micropayments** — Pay a fraction of a penny for a song, a poem, or a brushstroke. Artists can be paid for every single interaction.
- **Programmable Money** — Bitcoin can be set up with rules: automatic payments, time-locked savings, multi-signature security (like needing two keys to open a safe).
- **Borderless Commerce** — Sell to anyone, anywhere, without currency exchange fees or banking intermediaries.
- **Instant Settlement** — No waiting for checks, wire transfers, or payment processor approvals. Value moves at the speed of the internet.

**When people can economize better, human action becomes more productive — and everyone's quality of life improves.**

## Speaker Notes / Additional Talking Points

### On Barter
- Use relatable examples: trading lunch items at school, swapping toys with friends
- Emphasize that barter still exists today but it's limited — money made everything easier

### On Emergence
- Compare to language: nobody invented English in a meeting. It evolved over centuries from people communicating.
- Compare to trails in a park: the most popular paths get worn into the ground naturally.

### On Human Action
- Every student makes choices every day: what to eat, what to study, what to play. That's human action.
- The key insight: everyone's needs are different, and everyone's timeline is different. Money respects that diversity.

### On Free Markets
- Free markets aren't "no rules" — they're "no force." Fraud and theft are still wrong.
- The beauty is that prices communicate information that helps everyone make better decisions.

### On Bitcoin for Artists
- Artists are especially hurt by inflation because creative work takes time, and savings lose value under fiat.
- Bitcoin lets artists get paid instantly from anywhere in the world — no intermediary taking a cut.
- The 21-million cap means an artist's savings today will never be diluted by money printing.

### On Community
- Start small: even two people trading in Bitcoin creates a tiny circular economy.
- As more people join, the network effect grows — just like the internet became more valuable as more people connected.
