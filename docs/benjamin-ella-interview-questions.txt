Share Your Bitcoin Journey: Benjamin Ella — Interview Questions


1. Benjamin, most people know you as a Soloist of The Royal Ballet — 17 seasons, created roles for Wayne McGregor, Crystal Pite, Hofesh Shechter. For anyone who doesn't know your story, take us back to Melbourne — tennis player turned dancer at 11, obsessed with Carlos Acosta by 13, and a full scholarship to the Royal Ballet School at 15. What was it like leaving Australia as a teenager to chase this?

2. Your parents were both professional ballet dancers who ran their own school. You've talked about the blurred lines between parent and teacher, living in the studio, the financial pressures. How did growing up inside that world shape how you think about money and the economics of being an artist?

3. You retired from The Royal Ballet in December 2025 after 16 years. That's a major identity shift — what does life look like now? Are you pursuing choreography full time?

4. How did you first discover Bitcoin? Was there a specific moment or conversation that clicked for you?

5. You found Bitcoin For The Arts through Nostr. What brought you to Nostr in the first place? As a dancer in the classical world, that's not the typical path.

6. The ballet world is deeply institutional — Royal Opera House, Arts Council England, corporate sponsors. How does Bitcoin's philosophy of decentralization and permissionlessness land for someone who spent 17 years inside one of the most traditional institutions in the arts?

7. As a dancer, you lived on a fixed salary in London — one of the most expensive cities in the world. You've seen firsthand how inflation eats into an artist's purchasing power. Did that personal experience make Bitcoin's hard cap and sound money principles resonate differently for you?

8. You and I share something rare — we're both professional dancers who found Bitcoin. I danced with Ailey, DTH, Philadanco, and The Lion King. You danced with The Royal Ballet. Very few people in the Bitcoin space understand what it means to train your body for decades, perform 8 shows a week, and still worry about financial survival. How would you describe that tension between artistic excellence and economic reality?

9. You created roles in works by Crystal Pite, Wayne McGregor, Hofesh Shechter — some of the most innovative choreographers alive. Pite's Flight Pattern dealt with the refugee crisis. McGregor's The Dante Project explored suffering and transcendence. Do you see any philosophical parallels between the themes in these works and what Bitcoin represents?

10. You dealt with a serious foot injury early in your career — three years on and off, titanium pins, almost quit dancing in 2012. That resilience, that low-time-preference commitment to your craft despite short-term suffering — Bitcoiners call that "HODL mentality." Do you see a connection?

11. You've started exploring choreography — the piece for Northern Ballet, the Hikaru Kobayashi gala in Japan with Nunez, Ball, Magri, Muntagirov. As you build your choreographic career, do you see a role for Bitcoin and value-for-value in how dance gets funded and distributed?

12. Classical ballet relies almost entirely on institutional funding — government arts councils, corporate sponsors, wealthy patrons. What would a Bitcoin-native funding model look like for dance? Is it even possible?

13. Your Instagram says "Disciple." How does your faith intersect with your views on Bitcoin and artistic sovereignty? Some of the artists we've featured at BFTA talk about Bitcoin as a tool for moral alignment — honest money, honest work.

14. You grew up in Melbourne — same city as Matt Finlay, another BFTA featured artist who turned Melbourne's lockdowns into dissident music. Australia has a complicated relationship with Bitcoin — banks freezing accounts, strict regulations. What's your perspective on that from having grown up there?

15. If you could go back and talk to 18-year-old Benjamin Ella, just joining The Royal Ballet with a stress fracture and an Artist contract — what would you tell him about money, sovereignty, and planning for life after dance?

16. What would you say to a young dancer today who's curious about Bitcoin but thinks it's "not for artists" or "too technical"?

17. What's next for you? Where do you want to be in 5 years — and does Bitcoin play a role in that vision?

18. Last question — we ask everyone this on Share Your Bitcoin Journey: if a $500 to $2,000 Bitcoin micro-grant from BFTA landed in your wallet tomorrow, what would it unlock for you as a creator?
