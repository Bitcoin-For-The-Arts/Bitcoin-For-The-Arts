## Project Description

Bitcoin For The Arts (BFTA) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit building the first comprehensive, open-licensed Bitcoin education program designed specifically for working artists — visual artists, musicians, dancers, playwrights, writers, filmmakers, and storytellers.

All education materials are published under CC BY 4.0 in a dedicated public repository (github.com/Bitcoin-For-The-Arts/education) and delivered through free webinars, with no paywalls, no signups, and no invite-only gates.

The program currently includes two completed webinar modules:

1. **Introduction to Bitcoin For The Arts** — Why Bitcoin matters for artists, the economic case for arts funding ($1.17T arts GDP yet chronic creator underfunding), and how BFTA's 55/30/10/5 allocation model works.

2. **Bitcoin in Practice for Artists** — A hands-on session covering wallet setup (BlueWallet, Phoenix), self-custody, three methods for accepting Bitcoin for art (direct address, Lightning invoices, BTCPay Server), the Lightning Network for micro-payments and zaps, building a Bitcoin patron community, tax basics for Bitcoin-receiving artists, applying for a BFTA micro-grant ($500–$2,000), and security best practices.

The project website (bitcoinforthearts.org) is itself fully open-source (MIT-licensed Next.js/TypeScript application), integrates BTCPay Server for Bitcoin-native payments, and includes a first-party grant application system, volunteer signup, governance board nominations, and education workshop pages — all built transparently on GitHub.

This grant would fund the expansion of our education program over six months: developing four additional webinar modules, producing companion written guides, translating materials into Spanish and French, and building self-paced interactive tutorials — all published under CC BY 4.0 and freely accessible to anyone.

## Potential Impact

**For artists and the broader free and open-source community:**

1. **Onboarding a new demographic to Bitcoin.** Artists represent a vast, underserved population that stands to benefit enormously from Bitcoin (censorship-resistant payments, global reach, direct patronage) but lacks accessible entry points. Our education materials bridge that gap with practical, jargon-free instruction tailored to creative professionals.

2. **Open educational infrastructure.** Every module, slide deck, PDF, and guide we produce is licensed CC BY 4.0. Any educator, Bitcoin meetup, arts organization, or community group worldwide can freely use, remix, and redistribute these materials. This creates compounding value for the Bitcoin education ecosystem.

3. **Demonstrating Bitcoin-native nonprofit operations.** BFTA operates with radical on-chain transparency: a public treasury repo, 3-of-5 multisig HODL Vault, and a clear 55/30/10/5 allocation model. Our open-source website (MIT) serves as a reference implementation for how a nonprofit can run entirely on Bitcoin infrastructure (BTCPay Server, no fiat middlemen).

4. **Growing Bitcoin adoption through culture.** The arts contribute $1.17 trillion to U.S. GDP. If even a small fraction of that creative economy adopts Bitcoin for payments, patronage, and savings, the network effect is significant. Education is the catalyst.

5. **Developer-adjacent education.** While our primary audience is artists, the program includes technical content on Lightning Network usage, BTCPay Server deployment, Nostr integration, and self-custody tooling — all of which drive awareness and adoption of free and open-source Bitcoin developer tools.

### A personal note from the founder

I want to be upfront: Bitcoin For The Arts is early. We are a young organization without a long track record or big-name backers to point to. What we do have is real work, real materials, and a mission I believe in down to my bones.

I spent 16 years as a principal dancer — Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater, Dance Theatre of Harlem, Disney's The Lion King national tour. I've performed in over 20 countries and 45 U.S. states. I grew up in Baltimore, came up through the arts, and I know firsthand what it means to be a working artist surviving in a system that chronically underfunds the people who make the culture. I served as a union representative on The Lion King tour fighting for performers' rights. I helped negotiate the Ailey dancers' AGMA contract and reformed the pay scale for the first time since unionization. I've mentored over 10,000 students across the country — from the Baltimore School for the Arts to the American Ballet Theatre Summer Program to the Alvin Ailey Dance Center.

I started Bitcoin For The Arts because I watched the arts funding model break in real time. Artists depend on institutions that can freeze accounts, hold payments, censor work, and inflate away savings. I found Bitcoin, and I realized: this is the fix. Uncensorable money for uncensorable minds. Direct patronage with no middlemen. A reserve that can't be printed into nothing. Everything I wished existed when I was a young dancer from Bmore trying to build a life in the arts.

We don't have deep credibility yet in the Bitcoin open-source world — but we have conviction, and we are building in the open. Our website is MIT-licensed. Our education materials are CC BY 4.0. Our treasury is on-chain and publicly verifiable. We built our own grant application system, our own contact forms, our own governance tools — no Google Forms, no fiat processors, all auditable on GitHub. Every webinar we've delivered is free, every resource we've published is open, and every sat we receive follows a transparent allocation model.

I'm not asking for support based on reputation. I'm asking based on the work. The two webinars are published. The open-source codebase is live. The 501(c)(3) is filed. The board is seated. The education repo is public. We are doing this — and with dedicated funding for the education program, we can do it at the scale this community deserves.

The arts represent $1.17 trillion of U.S. GDP, and right now that entire world is almost completely absent from Bitcoin. This grant would help us change that — one artist, one webinar, one open-licensed module at a time.
