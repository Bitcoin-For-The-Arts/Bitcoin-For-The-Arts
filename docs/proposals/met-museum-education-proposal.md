Dear Ms. Holder and the Metropolitan Museum of Art Education Team,

Art has always moved faster than institutions. The Impressionists were rejected by the Salon. Jazz was dismissed as noise. Graffiti was criminalized before it was canonized. Today, a quiet revolution in money is reshaping how art is funded, owned, and valued — and the artists who understand it first will define the next creative era.

**Bitcoin For The Arts, Inc.** is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and the first organization in the world paying artists directly in Bitcoin. We are writing to propose a collaborative education webinar — or series — hosted in partnership with The Met's renowned education department, designed to equip working artists, educators, and creative professionals with practical financial literacy around Bitcoin and decentralized finance.

### Why This Matters — And Why Now

The arts contribute **$1.17 trillion to U.S. GDP**, yet the individual artist remains one of the most financially precarious workers in the American economy. Payment processors freeze accounts over controversial content. International commissions lose 5–15% to wire fees and exchange rates. Inflation silently erodes the modest savings artists manage to set aside. The tools of financial sovereignty have existed for over fifteen years — most artists simply haven't been introduced to them.

This is not speculative technology. Bitcoin is the largest, most secure decentralized monetary network in history, with a fixed supply of 21 million — a property that mirrors the scarcity and intentionality artists understand intuitively. The Metropolitan Museum itself sits at the intersection of art and enduring value. We believe your education programs are the ideal stage for this conversation.

### What We Propose

A **free, artist-centered webinar** (virtual, in-person, or hybrid) titled:

> **"Sound Money for Sound Art: Bitcoin Financial Literacy for the Creative Professional"**

This is not a pitch for speculation. This is a practical workshop covering:

**Module 1 — Why Bitcoin Belongs in the Artist's Toolkit**
- The economics of creative work: how artists get paid, where the money goes, and what's broken
- Bitcoin fundamentals in plain language: fixed supply, decentralization, censorship resistance
- Historical parallels: from patronage to platforms to permissionless money
- Case studies: international artists bypassing broken banking systems; photographers whose accounts were frozen for political work; sculptors preserving grant value against inflation

**Module 2 — Hands-On: From Theory to Practice**
- Setting up a self-custodial Bitcoin wallet (live, step-by-step)
- Receiving your first sats (the smallest unit of Bitcoin)
- Understanding self-custody: "Not your keys, not your coins" — and why that's artistic sovereignty
- Three methods for accepting Bitcoin for creative work: direct address sharing, Lightning Network invoices, and BTCPay Server (the self-hosted, zero-fee payment processor)

**Module 3 — The New Patronage Economy**
- Building a global patron community without platform middlemen
- The Lightning Network: instant, near-zero-fee micro-payments (imagine getting tipped during a livestream, or selling a $5 print without losing 30 cents + 2.9% to a processor)
- Tools: BTCPay Server, Nostr (decentralized social media with built-in tipping), Geyser Fund (Bitcoin-native crowdfunding), Lightning Addresses, Podcasting 2.0
- The "100 True Fans" model: how 100 supporters sending ~$10/month in Bitcoin can sustain a practice

**Module 4 — Protecting Your Work and Your Wealth**
- Tax basics for artists receiving Bitcoin (Bitcoin is classified as property by the IRS)
- Record-keeping best practices
- Security essentials: seed phrases, hardware wallets, recognizing scams
- A pathway to applying for Bitcoin For The Arts micro-grants ($500–$2,000, paid directly to the artist's wallet)

### What Makes This Different

This is not a crypto marketing event. This is **arts education** rooted in economic history and delivered through the lens of creative practice. Our curriculum was developed by and for working artists. We hold ourselves to radical transparency:

- **55% of all donations** go directly to artist grants
- **30%** fund programs like this one
- **10%** cover operations
- **5%** seed a long-term Bitcoin reserve (our "HODL Vault") — building a permanent endowment for human creativity that no institution or inflation can erode

All of our educational materials are **open-source and licensed under CC BY 4.0** — freely available for The Met's educators to adapt, redistribute, and build upon.

We are the first nonprofit paying artists in Bitcoin, funding sovereign creators across visual arts, theater, dance, music, writing, storytelling, and film. Our treasury is publicly verifiable on the Bitcoin blockchain — a standard of financial transparency that no traditional arts funder has matched.

### What We're Asking

1. **A conversation.** We would welcome the opportunity to present this proposal in greater detail — by phone, video, or in person at The Met.
2. **A pilot session.** A single 90-minute webinar for artists and educators in your network, offered at no cost, to demonstrate the value and relevance of this material.
3. **An ongoing partnership.** If the pilot resonates, we envision a recurring series integrated into The Met's educator programming — building the most comprehensive artist-focused Bitcoin literacy program at any major cultural institution in the world.

### The Bigger Picture

The Metropolitan Museum of Art has spent over 150 years preserving humanity's creative legacy. Bitcoin, at its core, is a technology for preserving value across time — incorruptible, uninflatable, and owned by no one. The parallel is not accidental.

The artists who shaped history — from the anonymous builders of Egyptian tombs to the Renaissance masters funded by the Medici — all operated within the monetary systems of their age. The monetary system is changing again. The question is not whether artists will encounter Bitcoin, but whether they will understand it when they do.

We believe The Met's education programs are uniquely positioned to ensure they do.

We would be honored to discuss this further at your convenience. Thank you for your time, your mission, and your extraordinary commitment to education in the arts.
