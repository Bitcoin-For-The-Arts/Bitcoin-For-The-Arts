Share Your Bitcoin Journey: Episode 5 — Petra (@Petrahodl)
Interview Questions


BACKGROUND & JOURNEY

1. Petra, welcome to Share Your Bitcoin Journey. You've had a fascinating path — born in South Africa, educated in Sweden across three universities, studied peace and development, international relations, and information security. You've lived and worked in Spain, Ecuador, Nicaragua, and Sweden. For anyone who doesn't know you, tell us about that journey and how all those experiences shaped who you are today.

2. Your academic background is in peace and development studies and political science at Linnaeus University and Uppsala University, and then you pivoted to a master's in information security at Stockholm University. That's a big shift. What prompted that move, and looking back, do you see a thread connecting peace studies and information security?

3. You speak Swedish, English, and Spanish at a native or bilingual level. You've worked as a translator, including for PlanB Network — one of the most important Bitcoin education platforms in the world. What was it like translating Bitcoin educational content, and how did that deepen your own understanding?


THE BITCOIN JOURNEY

4. When did you first encounter Bitcoin? Was there a specific moment, a conversation, a book, or something in your life experience that made it click?

5. Your LinkedIn activity tells a story — you're engaging with content about women in Bitcoin, Bitcoin and human rights, Bitcoin education, financial literacy. It's clear this isn't just about price for you. What does Bitcoin represent to you philosophically?

6. You grew up in South Africa — a country with a complicated economic history, currency instability, and deep inequality. How does that background shape how you see Bitcoin's potential, especially for people in the Global South?

7. You worked at Worldline Global in financial services for over two years — technical support for a major payments company. Then you went down the Bitcoin rabbit hole. What was it like seeing the traditional financial system from the inside and then discovering an alternative?


ZAPRANA — SOVEREIGNTY IN HEALING

8. Let's talk about Zaprana. Your tagline is "Sovereignty in Healing." You're building a platform that connects wellness practitioners and clients, with payments in sats. Tell us the vision — what is Zaprana and why does it need to exist?

9. The name Zaprana — it combines "zap" from the Bitcoin/Lightning world with something that feels like prana, the Sanskrit word for life force or breath. Is that intentional? What's the meaning behind the name?

10. You've studied herbalism and holistic health coaching for women. How do you see the connection between physical sovereignty — taking control of your own health and healing — and financial sovereignty through Bitcoin?

11. Zaprana launched through Geyser Fund, which is a Bitcoin-native crowdfunding platform. What was that experience like? How has the Bitcoin community responded to the idea of wellness meets sound money?

12. A lot of wellness practitioners are independent — they're essentially solo artists running their own practices, often underbanked, often operating peer-to-peer. That sounds a lot like the independent artist problem that Bitcoin For The Arts is trying to solve. Do you see that parallel?


WOMEN IN BITCOIN

13. Your LinkedIn shows you engaging heavily with the women-in-Bitcoin community — SatoShe, conversations about why women feel left out of Bitcoin, financial literacy for women. What's your experience been as a woman in this space?

14. Do you think the wellness and holistic health community — which skews heavily female — is a natural entry point for women into Bitcoin? Is that part of what you're building with Zaprana?


BITCOIN, ART & CREATIVITY

15. At Bitcoin For The Arts, we define "art" broadly — music, dance, visual art, film, writing, but also the art of healing, the art of craft, the art of building something sovereign. Do you see what you're doing with Zaprana as a creative act?

16. You worked at Svadia, an arts and crafts company in Stockholm, doing translation and marketing. You've been around creative industries. What do you think artists and wellness practitioners have in common when it comes to the economic challenges they face?

17. Bitcoin For The Arts is building a model where artists get paid directly in Bitcoin — no middlemen, no gatekeepers. Zaprana is building the same thing for wellness practitioners. If these two communities connected, what could that look like?


LOOKING FORWARD

18. You're building Zaprana, you're connected to PlanB Network, you're active on Nostr and in the women-in-Bitcoin community. What does the next year look like for you? Where is this all heading?

19. What would you say to a wellness practitioner or a healer who's never heard of Bitcoin but is tired of payment processors taking a cut, tired of platforms controlling their client relationships, tired of the system not working for them?

20. Last question — we ask everyone this on Share Your Bitcoin Journey: if a $500 to $2,000 Bitcoin micro-grant from Bitcoin For The Arts landed in your wallet tomorrow, what would it unlock for you as a creator and builder?
